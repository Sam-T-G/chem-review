# D-0033: Step 5 asks for the electron geometry, then the molecular geometry, and every sentence uses those two names

- Status: provisional
- Date: 2026-11-17
- Decided by: The build session (package FLOW), reading D-0030
- Teams: lewis, learning

## Context
Sam asked on 2026-11-17 to "incorporate particularly differentiating between electron and molecular geometry, along with all of the appropriate applications through the entire app stack to make way for this", and to "enable 3d models for molecules that do not have a center element". D-0030 quotes him in full. The app said "arrangement" and "shape"; the course says "electron geometry" and "molecular geometry". How the guided steps, the cards and free build carry the two names is this session's design, and Sam has not confirmed it.

Options looked at for step 5:
- Ask only the molecular geometry and show the electron geometry in the table. Turned down: the student never has to tell the two apart, which is what Sam asked for.
- Make the electron geometry's first pick final, as the molecular geometry's is. Turned down: nothing on the board shows the electron geometry when it is picked, so a final first pick would give the answer before the student has worked it out (L3).
- Make the electron geometry card a list of all five names with their counts. Turned down: with the group count already on screen, the list answers the open question (L10).

## Decision
- Step 5 asks three questions in order: the number of electron groups, the electron geometry, then the molecular geometry (the existing choices). The picks for the electron geometry are the five electron geometries and, with them, the molecular geometries that the same number of electron groups can make (`geometryPicks()`: for 4 groups, trigonal pyramidal and bent; for 2 groups, bent; for 6 groups, square planar alone, because a seventh pick takes a fourth row on a phone and the picks then stand taller than the panel), so a student can give a molecular geometry where the electron geometry is asked. The extra names follow the count of groups alone, which is already on screen, so they say nothing about the molecule's lone pairs. The lead added them on 2026-11-17 after a review found that the five names alone let that mistake be made on 1 of the 20 molecules. The model lifts into 3D after the molecular geometry, as before.
- A wrong electron geometry is explained in three parts and the student picks again. The first miss names the wrong idea: a name that only a molecular geometry has ("Bent is a molecular geometry: it describes where the atoms are and leaves the lone pairs out.", with the five electron geometries listed and none picked out), the electron geometry for the bonded atoms alone, the electron geometry for a count that takes each shared pair of a double or triple bond as a group, the middle atom counted as a group, a full shell of 8 read as 4 groups (tetrahedral picked where the middle atom has another count and not 8 electrons), or else what the pick goes with. The second miss keeps that idea and hands back a smaller question: the count as bonded atoms and lone pairs, and which of the five electron geometries spreads that many groups as far apart as they can get. The third names the pick, gives the rule (2 groups is linear, 3 is trigonal planar, 4 is tetrahedral, 5 is trigonal bipyramidal, 6 is octahedral) as the reason, and then the answer and the pick to make. The first build gave the rule on the second miss and as the second hint; with the count already on screen that is the answer, and a review caught it (COPY2-2, fixed 2026-11-17). A miss about lone pairs or double bonds rings them on the board. No card for the electron geometry says which name is the molecule's molecular geometry: that question comes next and its first pick is final. The first build had such a card for xenon difluoride ("Linear is where the 2 atoms bonded to xenon sit: the molecular geometry.") and a review caught it. The same wrong pick pressed again is not a new try. The right-answer lines say which geometry they name: "The electron geometry is tetrahedral: 4 electron groups around oxygen." and "The molecular geometry is bent: 2 bonded atoms and 2 lone pairs around oxygen."
- `geometryFeedback(center, info, pick)` keeps its signature for the shapes quiz and the exercises. Two optional arguments were added at the end, `tries` and the board's group facts, so the guided step can hold the answer back and detect the double-bond miscount. Without them the call answers as a first miss and cannot tell a double bond counted twice from any other miscount.
- Every sentence a student reads says "electron geometry" or "molecular geometry" where it means one of them: the questions, the right-answer lines, the cards for wrong answers, the hints, the table of results (rows: Molecular geometry, Electron geometry, Electron groups, Bond angle), step 6's reasons and the key terms' cards. "Shape" stays as step 5's short label and in "See the 3D shape".
- Key terms: "Electron geometry" has a new card (id `electron-geometry`). The card with id `shape` is now "Molecular geometry", and both "molecular geometry" and "shape" open it. Both cards work on nitrogen trifluoride, bromine trifluoride or iodine pentafluoride, whichever has another number of groups and another molecular geometry than the molecule on screen. In step 5 the names of the geometries stay plain until the molecular geometry is picked.
- A molecule of two atoms (an exercise can be one): both feedback functions answer it with plain lines ("The molecular geometry is linear: 2 atoms always sit on a straight line." and "The electron geometry is linear: for a molecule of 2 atoms it is given the same name as the molecular geometry."). The straight line is the reason for the molecular geometry only; the electron geometry's lines state the course's name and give no reason drawn from where the atoms sit, and its misses before the third ask "Which name fits 2 atoms joined by a bond?" without the answer (CHEM2-4, COPY2-6). No line names a middle atom or a count of groups, because `ShapeInfo.groups` for two atoms counts around one of them (the lead's note from package CHEM, 2026-11-17).
- A key term's card never uses the molecule on screen as its example, for an exercise too. Every card's example list has a second molecule, and the picker skips a sample whose formula and charge are the molecule on screen. Eight samples were added for this: BeF2, CS2, AsF5, SeCl4, ICl3, PF6−, ClF5, BrF4− (the lead's note, 2026-11-17).
- Free build, a finished molecule of two atoms: "There is no middle atom. The hydrogen atom and the chlorine atom sit on a straight line, so the molecular geometry is linear and there is no bond angle. The electron geometry is given as linear too." The straight line is the reason for the molecular geometry only: whether the course calls the electron geometry of such a molecule linear is the open question below. The 3D table drops the electron groups, the bond is named with the less electronegative atom first, and the one bond's dipole is the net dipole. The guided list keeps one middle atom per problem.

## Consequences
- `src/modules/lewis/guide.ts` (`groupsQuestion`, `geometryQuestion`, `shapeQuestion`, `geometryFeedback`, `geometryRight`, `twoAtomsLine`, `plainTerms`, the two cards), `src/modules/lewis/store.ts` (`pickGeometry`, the `geometry*` fields of `ShapeState`), `src/modules/lewis/ui.ts` (`stepShape`, `readout`, `freePanel`). Tests: `tests/guide.test.ts` checks every claim of every electron geometry card for all twenty problems and all 13 rows of the shape table, `tests/terms.test.ts` holds the two cards to L10, `tests/store.test.ts` walks the new question. `scripts/check-browser.mjs` answers it with real input in every guided run and checks a wrong pick, a molecule of two atoms and the two cards.
- Calling the electron geometry of a molecule of two atoms "linear" follows the run brief for D-0030. Some textbooks would give chlorine in HCl a tetrahedral electron-pair geometry (1 bond and 3 lone pairs). Sam or the chem team should confirm which reading the course uses, and `docs/chemistry/sources.md` should cite it.
- Open: the step split and the card's three parts stay provisional (D-0007, D-0017).
